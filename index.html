A sample weekly schedule for college + JAM

This is an illustrative plan for a student attending regular B.Sc. classes, with roughly 2–3 focused hours on weekdays and longer blocks at the weekend. Consistency matters more than the exact hours.

  • Monday — New concept90 min: one sub-topic from your current subject (e.g. central force motion). Write the key derivation yourself. 30 min: 5–8 textbook problems.
  • Tuesday — Mathematical Methods60 min: a maths tool you need this week (matrices, Fourier series, ODEs). 60 min: problems that apply it to a physics context.
  • Wednesday — Continue + PYQs60 min: finish Monday’s topic. 45 min: previous-year questions on that topic only, untimed, with full working.
  • Thursday — Second subject90 min: a lighter or complementary subject (e.g. Kinetic Theory and Thermodynamics, or Modern Physics) so you rotate through the syllabus.
  • Friday — Short revision45 min: rewrite formulas and results from the week on one page. 45 min: re-solve problems you got wrong.
  • Saturday — Timed practice2–3 hrs: a sectional or topic test under exam-like timing (MCQ, MSQ and NAT styles). Note every mistake.
  • Sunday — Review and plan60–90 min: classify the week’s mistakes (concept, calculation, time). Plan next week. Keep part of the day free to rest.

Frequently asked questions

When should I begin preparing for IIT JAM Physics?

Many students begin seriously in the second year of B.Sc., which leaves time to cover the syllabus once, practise, and revise before the final-year exam. Starting in final year is still workable if you plan tightly and prioritise high-weight fundamentals. Check the current exam schedule on the official JAM 2027 important-dates page.

How should I use previous-year questions (PYQs)?

Use them in two stages. While learning a topic, solve that topic’s PYQs untimed to see how concepts are actually tested. Later, attempt full past papers under timed conditions as mock tests. Pravegaa provides IIT JAM Physics previous-year papers and step-by-step PYQ solutions free; official papers are also on the JAM website’s download page.

When should I start full-length mock tests?

Start topic and sectional tests as soon as you finish a subject. Move to full-length mocks once most of the syllabus has been covered once — typically in the last few months — and review each mock as carefully as you took it. The roadmap’s mock-review method shows how.

Should I choose a full course or only a test series?

Choose a full course if your main problem is understanding — you cannot solve standard problems without looking at solutions. Choose a test series if you understand the material but lose marks under time pressure. If you are unsure, take one timed PYQ paper first and look at where you lost marks, or ask a Pravegaa mentor.

Not sure which path fits you?

Tell a Pravegaa mentor your year of study, how much of the syllabus you have covered, and how your last practice test went. You will get a clear recommendation for your next step.
