How to Prepare for IIT JAM Physics

A four-stage plan you can run alongside college: build the foundation, learn the exam through previous-year questions, train under time pressure, and revise until your mistakes stop repeating.

Know what you are preparing for

According to the official JAM 2027 syllabus and test-pattern page (organised by IIT Kharagpur), the exam is a computer-based test with three sections:

  • Section A — multiple choice questions (MCQ), with negative marking for wrong answers.
  • Section B — multiple select questions (MSQ), with no negative marking.
  • Section C — numerical answer type (NAT) questions, entered using a virtual keyboard.

The official Physics (PH) syllabus is organised into seven areas: Mathematical Methods; Mechanics and General Properties of Matter; Oscillations, Waves and Optics; Electricity and Magnetism; Kinetic Theory and Thermodynamics; Modern Physics; and Solid State Physics, Devices and Electronics. Read the full PDF once and keep it as your coverage map. Dates and eligibility are published on the official important-dates page.

Stage 1: Foundation

The goal is not to “finish” chapters but to be able to solve standard problems without looking at the solution.

  • Start with Mathematical Methods. Vectors, matrices, differential equations and Fourier methods appear inside almost every other area. Weakness here shows up everywhere.
  • Learn each topic in a fixed order: concept → derivation in your own handwriting → worked examples → 10–15 problems on your own.
  • Keep a one-page summary per topic with key results, limiting cases and units. You will revise from these later.
  • Pair heavy and light subjects in the same week so you keep moving through the syllabus.

If you prefer guided notes, Pravegaa’s free physics study material is organised topic-wise.

Stage 2: Previous-year questions

Previous-year questions (PYQs) show you how concepts are actually tested — which results are used, how options are designed to trap you, and how much calculation is typical.

  1. Topic-wise, untimed: after finishing a topic, solve every PYQ on it. Write full working.
  2. Check, don’t copy: compare with a worked solution only after a genuine attempt. Note what idea you missed.
  3. Full papers, timed: later, attempt complete past papers as mocks (see Stage 3).

Use the IIT JAM Physics previous-year question papers and PYQ solutions from Pravegaa, or download official papers from the JAM website.

Stage 3: Timed practice

Knowing physics and scoring in a timed paper are different skills. Train the second one deliberately.

  • Sectional tests first: after each subject, take a short timed test on it.
  • Practise each question type: MCQs need judgement about when to skip because of negative marking; MSQs need every correct option identified; NATs need accurate calculation with the on-screen calculator.
  • Full-length mocks: once most of the syllabus is covered, take full mocks at the same time of day as the real exam, in one sitting.
  • Every mock gets a review at least as long as the test itself.

Need structured mocks? Pravegaa’s IIT JAM Physics test series offers timed tests you can take alongside your own study.

Stage 4: Revision

  • Revise from your own summaries, not from full textbooks.
  • Re-solve your error log — problems you got wrong are the best revision material you have.
  • Spaced cycles: revisit each area every 2–3 weeks rather than once at the end.
  • In the final weeks, stop starting new topics; consolidate and keep taking mocks.

Beginner or repeat aspirant? Adapt the plan

How the four stages shift
StageBeginner (first attempt)Repeat aspirant
FoundationMost of your time. Cover every area once, in order, starting with Mathematical Methods.Only the areas where last year’s scores or mocks were weak. Don’t restart from zero.
PYQsTopic-wise after each chapter.Start early with full timed papers to find gaps quickly.
Timed practiceSectional tests first; full mocks once most of the syllabus is done.Regular full mocks from early on; review each one in depth.
RevisionBuild summaries as you go; revise in cycles.Focus on your error log and recurring mistake types.

For repeaters: before planning, list why last attempt fell short — unfinished syllabus, weak accuracy, or running out of time. Each points to a different fix: more foundation work, more PYQ practice, or more timed mocks.

How to review a mock test

After every mock, go through each question you got wrong, skipped, or guessed. Put each into one of three buckets:

Mistake typeHow to spot itWhat to do next
Concept gapYou could not start, or used the wrong principle.Re-study that sub-topic, then solve 5–10 fresh problems on it.
Calculation mistakeRight method, wrong number — sign, factor of 2, units, approximation.Re-solve without help; write down the exact slip; practise similar NATs.
Time-management errorYou knew it but didn’t reach it, or spent too long on one question.Set a time limit per question; practise skipping and returning.

Keep a simple running log: date, mock, question, bucket, and the one-line fix. After three or four mocks, count the buckets. The largest bucket tells you what to change next week.

Progress checklist

Use this every month to see where you stand.

  • I have read the official Physics syllabus and marked each area as done, in progress or not started.
  • I can solve standard textbook problems in Mathematical Methods without help.
  • I have a one-page summary for every topic I have finished.
  • I have solved the previous-year questions for every finished topic.
  • I have taken at least one timed test this month and reviewed it fully.
  • My mistake log shows fewer calculation and time errors than last month.
  • I know which areas I am weakest in, and they are in next week’s plan.
